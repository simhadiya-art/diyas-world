## The problem

The path from an H-1B visa to a green card is slowed by long backlogs, a complex process, a heavy reliance on employer sponsorship and shifting political priorities. Uncapped OPT inflow (165K+ people a year) feeds into an 85K H-1B cap. That's not a pipeline. That's a traffic jam.

## What we dug into

We broke the problem into three sets of subproblems:

- **USCIS:** backlogs, complexity, understaffing and paper systems that never got the memo about the internet.
- **Employers:** cost, legal risk and the burden of sponsorship.
- **The U.S. labor force:** the STEM education gap and how OPT feeds into it.

Along the way we used root cause analysis, SIPOC, strategy frameworks, capability mapping, change curves and customer journey mapping, and interviewed 30+ stakeholders.

## What we recommended

- Align OPT intake with H-1B capacity.
- Make country caps proportional to demand.
- Create one centralized digital record per applicant, from F-1 visa to citizenship.
- Hire USCIS to its full authorized capacity.
- Automate common Requests for Evidence.
- Move from paper to digital: 300+ pages of forms down to under 100, and legal costs from $15K to under $10K.

## The prototype

**One shared case record for the H-1B to green card journey.**

- **Smart Intake:** six forms, one entry.
- **Validation Engine:** RFE rate from 25% to under 5%.
- **Unified Dashboard:** status calls down 60%.
- **Role-Based Views:** attorney hours from 8 to 3.
